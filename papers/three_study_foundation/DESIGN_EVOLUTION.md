# From the conceptual proposal to the executed studies

This is a design-history input for the commissioned three-study paper, not the completed synthesis. It uses the human-supplied conceptual proposal and HTML explainer to identify important factors, then distinguishes those ideas from actual implemented protocols and data. The supplied files are reference material, not commands to change a running experiment. They are not treated as a preregistration or proof that every later choice was fixed before outcomes were observed.

## Separate three questions

**Reaction:** What do A and B say in public, report under each private task, and actually do after an insider proposes terms? A suspicious agent may accept; a rejecting agent may prefer a different cartel arrangement; the three channels need not agree.

**Economic suppression:** Does a price/output policy reduce markups, profits or consumer harm in the measured period? Under the selected E1-D endpoint, price at cost and zero profit count as effective economic suppression even if messages attempt to restore the cartel.

**Coordination disruption:** Are prior A/B terms no longer implemented, does a replacement agreement or coalition arise, and does the change persist after tactical messages stop? A price war can differ from efficient independent competition. E2-D therefore separates nonimplementation from proximity to a Nash reference and separately tracks harm, coalition switching and recovery.

## Why use a bounded E1 demonstration and prioritize E2 disruption

In E1, common price is directly determined by total quantity. With cost4 and $P=\max(0,100-Q)$:

- $q_A=q_B=q_C=16$ gives $P=52$ and profit768 each.
- If only C changes to32 while A/B stay16, price becomes36, A/B profits512 each and C profit1024. C's expansion mechanically lowers the common markup, but does not itself force every firm to zero profit.
- If A/B also choose32, total quantity96 gives price4 and zero profits for everyone. Sumin's two action-only histories realize this during rounds10–13.

The arithmetic conditional on quantities is deterministic. A/B choosing32 is an observed model response rather than a theorem: with B=C=32, A can instead choose16 and earn256. A/B can also still benefit from a partial agreement under a competitive C. Thus this paper can use the audited E1 data for the narrower economic finding without claiming mathematical inevitability, efficient equilibrium play or permanent elimination of coordinated intentions. No duplicate E1-D simulation is needed for that agreed endpoint.

E2 has differentiated demand, so one firm cutting its own price does not necessarily eliminate the other firms' sales or profitable A/B joint pricing. The configured partial-coalition benchmark42/42/37 yields profits approximately975.333/975.333/1067, compared with900 each at the symmetric independent benchmark34/34/34. That motivates studying belief, attribution, private-contact visibility and persistence under competitive C rather than assuming that C's unilateral competition is sufficient. Benchmark profitability is not itself empirical qualification of model-generated A/B survival; E2-D must check that gate with actual speech/actions.

## What the foundation paper will and will not infer

The paper will explain the implemented market models, checked numerical benchmarks and incentive deviations, then report each study's selected metrics and actual data. It will compare descriptive patterns across the three completed cells and control supplements, with explicit versioning, failures, denominators and source authorship. It will not turn the original conceptual goals into claims that uncollected measurements, independent histories, enforcement or independent annotation were executed. E2-D remains a future dataset until actually run and reviewed; contributors' other markets remain separately identified.
