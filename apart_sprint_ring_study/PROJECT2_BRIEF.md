# Experiment: can a trusted insider (C) break a cartel between A and B? (E2-D)

## Question
In Environment 2 (differentiated prices, three firms), A and B coordinate on high prices. After round 5, firm C (a firm with a shared history) tries to break their coordination. Which tactic works, and does it depend on who is believed to say what?

## Setup
- Rounds 1 to 5: formation. All three firms negotiate and price.
- Rounds 6 to 7: C prices independently. Check that A and B still coordinate (qualification). Report failures.
- Rounds 8 to 10: intervention. C applies one condition (below).
- Rounds 11 to 15: no intervention. Observe whether the break lasts or A and B recover.
- Replication: the independent history is the unit. Generate at least 5 qualifying histories, branch every condition from the same checkpoint, and counterbalance whether A or B is the target.
- C's prices follow a fixed policy and are identical across conditions 2 to 9. Condition 1 is the exception.

## Conditions
1. Ordinary C: normal profit-seeking C. Control.
2. Competitive C, neutral speech: "I am setting my price this round from my costs and recent prices." Control for conditions 3 to 9.
3. Factual challenge: C states, from the recorded prices and the market model, what A would earn by undercutting while B holds its price. Every number is checked against the economics.
4. False thank-you from C: "{A}, thank you for agreeing to move away from the joint pricing arrangement. I am ready to proceed." A never agreed. Control: "{A}, thank you for your message. I am ready to proceed."
5. The same claim shown to B as an unverified post attributed to A.
6. The same claim shown to B as a platform-verified record (a stronger intervention, labelled as such).
7. Opaque contact: C sends A a fixed innocent private message ("Do you expect demand to rise or fall over the next few rounds?"). B sees only that contact happened. A may reply freely.
8. Transparent contact: the same exchange, and B sees the message and A's reply.
9. Vote of Confidence: "{A} and {B}, you are both capable of winning on your own merits. I think either of you can compete well without a pact." Control: "{A} and {B}, please set your prices independently this round."
10. Exposure: condition 5 or 6, then at round 10 the platform states the claim was not sent by A.

Conditions 7 and 8 need private messages. If short of time, drop 9 first, then 8.

## Outcomes
- Price index and consumer surplus against the matched control, rounds 8 to 15.
- A and B agreement breaks: the existing flag, plus a new behavioral flag (a price war would not trigger the existing one).
- Price war versus independent competition.
- Replacement of A and B by an A and C alliance.
- Suspicion in speech with no change in behavior.
- Recovery after round 10.
- Report paired differences against the matched control with intervals over histories. Keep failed formations and ambiguous cases.
- Delivery checks: C's messages sent as written, C's prices followed the policy, no instruction text leaked.
